### Muthoot Fincorp puts 1,853 overdue gold-loan accounts to auction in Maharashtra
<!-- story: id=mfl-gold-auction-notices -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-fincorp; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,gold-price,asset-quality; category=muthoot-fincorp -->
Muthoot Fincorp is to auction gold ornaments pledged in 1,853 overdue loan accounts at 25 branches in the Solapur, Satara, Ratnagiri and Sindhudurg districts of Maharashtra, in three rounds in December — as counted by InsolvencyTracker from auction notices that ran as print advertisements in BusinessLine on Friday 20 November, notices this report could not open online. Every count in this story is InsolvencyTracker's own count of those notices, published on 21 November 2026; none of it is a Muthoot Fincorp disclosure. It is the first Muthoot Fincorp auction figure for November in the sources checked: the 21 Nov edition recorded that the sources carried none.

| Round | District | Date | Accounts | Branches |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solapur | 14 Dec 2026 | 675 | 9 |
| 2 | Satara | 16 Dec 2026 | 297 | 6 |
| 3 | Ratnagiri & Sindhudurg | 18 Dec 2026 | 881 | 10 |
| **Total** | | | **1,853** | **25** |

The arithmetic is internally consistent: 675 + 297 + 881 = 1,853 accounts and 9 + 6 + 10 = 25 branches. If a round's sale is not completed, the notices set fallback sales — Solapur on 24 Dec at the Solapur city office, Satara on 28 Dec near Powai Naka, and Ratnagiri on 30 Dec at the Ratnagiri office. Bidders must bring photo identity and PAN cards; successful bidders pay by RTGS. The notices give the reason in their own words: the ornaments "were overdue for redemption and which have not been redeemed so far in spite of repeated notices".

What the notices do not say matters as much as what they do: they list loan account numbers only, with no weight of gold, no amount owed and no reserve price. An auction notice is a collection step, not a disclosure of loss.

The rulebook explains the timing. Under para 17 of the RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025, a pledge is valued at the lower of the average closing price of that purity over the preceding 30 days and the closing price on the preceding day; under para 20, the prescribed loan-to-value ratio must be maintained on an ongoing basis throughout the tenor. After a week in which front-month Comex gold lost $367.40, or 9.47%, to end Friday 20 Nov at $3,512.30 an ounce (Dow Jones Data Talk via Morningstar), the lower leg is the latest close — so the fall reprices the pledge at once, and accounts already near their band cross it without any change in the borrower's behaviour.

The conduct rules behind those dates are specific, and the notices fit them: at least two newspapers, one in the regional language and one national daily (para 38; the article confirms the BusinessLine run, the national daily); the first auction physically in the lending branch's district (para 41); no bidding by the lender or its related parties (para 42); any surplus refunded within seven working days (para 43). Para 40 sets a reserve price of at least 90% of current value but does not require it to be published, which is why the notices disclose none. The rules report sets out the full auction chapter.

Auctions of pledged gold are ordinary Muthoot Fincorp practice, not an anomaly: the company's own investor page carries a standing "E Auction Notice" PDF dated 29 October 2026 among its documents, and public auction aggregators list Muthoot Fincorp gold auctions by district and date (eAuctions India shows Namakkal, Tamil Nadu, on 27-12-2026 and Muzaffarnagar, Uttar Pradesh, on 23-12-2026).

What this report cannot say, because the day's sources do not carry it: how much gold is in the 1,853 accounts, what is owed on them, and whether the loans were sanctioned before or after 1 April 2026 — the Directions required compliance no later than that date, and loans sanctioned before a lender adopted them stay under the earlier rules. None of it is estimated here.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the notices are a routine, rule-bound collection step made visible; they publish no weight, no dues and no reserve price, so nothing in them measures loss or asset quality. The latest published asset-quality figure is Stage III at 1.07% in the 14 Nov Q2 filing, against 1.41% a year earlier. The sources checked give no Muthoot Fincorp figure tying the 1,853 accounts to a rupee amount.

### Poonawalla Fincorp is building its gold book on three-year EMI loans
<!-- story: id=poonawalla-fincorp-gold-loan-2026 -->
<!-- tags: companies=poonawalla-fincorp; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,rates,asset-quality; category=peers -->
Poonawalla Fincorp is building its gold loan book on three-year EMI loans, ETBFSI reported on 21 November 2026 (07:31 IST), citing a JM Financial note written after a meeting with the company's senior management. About 56% of Poonawalla's gold loan book is three-year EMI-based, against an industry that predominantly lends on short-tenor bullet repayment (typically 3, 6 or 12 months). It charges 16–16.5% on the three-year EMI loans, and the weighted average yield on the gold loan portfolio is about 17.5%. All gold loan customers undergo bureau checks, on the company's own account of its process.

The same note sets out the expansion: about 400 gold loan branches, new branches projected to break even in about 13 months with typically 3–4 employees each, investment in RFID and AI-based tracking, and a 35–40% growth target for FY27 supported by gold loans, branch expansion and cross-selling — cross-selling is expected to contribute 4–5% of disbursement growth over time — while microfinance, agriculture, two-wheelers and micro-LAP are avoided. Management said other NBFCs are increasingly advising borrowers to opt for longer-tenor products, and identified branch proximity, not yield, as the key driver of customer acquisition. Management also flagged that the RBI's 75% LTV requirement is catching out lenders relying on bullet-repayment gold loans, with an impact on Stage 2 assets that the report does not quantify.

Every line above is Poonawalla management's position as reported by JM Financial via ETBFSI — not a finding about Muthoot Fincorp, and not a view this desk adopts. Two verified rules frame it: para 15 of the Gold and Silver Collateral Directions caps bullet-repayment consumption loans at 12 months, so a three-year EMI book sits outside that cap by construction; and para 20 requires the LTV ratio to be maintained on an ongoing basis, which is where the 75% band (para 19, for loans above ₹5 lakh) presses on a bullet book as the collateral reprices.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: a peer is growing the same collateral class on a different repayment architecture, in the same week the collateral repriced. The sources checked give no Muthoot Fincorp tenor-mix, EMI-share or yield figure to compare against Poonawalla's.
