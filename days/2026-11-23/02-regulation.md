### The standing rulebook: RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025
<!-- reg: ref=RBI/2025-26/47 DOR.CRE.REC.26/21.01.023/2025-26; regulator=RBI; issued=2025-06-06; effective=2026-04-01; status=in-force; lifecycle=effective; applies=nbfcs -->
<!-- story: id=reg-rbi-rbi-2025-26-47 -->
The Directions were issued on 6 June 2025 (RBI/2025-26/47 DOR.CRE.REC.26/21.01.023/2025-26), updated on 29 September 2025 by the (1st Amendment) Directions (RBI/2025-26/84 DOR.CRE.REC.52/21.01.023/2025-26); compliance was required by 1 April 2026, and loans sanctioned before a lender adopted them stay under the earlier rules. Para 15 caps bullet-repayment consumption loans at 12 months, renewable under para 11; para 16 caps ornaments at 1 kg of gold (10 kg silver) and coins at 50 g gold (500 g silver) per borrower; para 17 values collateral at the lower of the average closing price of that purity over the preceding 30 days and the closing price on the preceding day, per IBJA or a SEBI-regulated commodity exchange; para 19 sets maximum LTV at 85% (loans up to ₹2.5 lakh), 80% (₹2.5–5 lakh) and 75% (above ₹5 lakh); para 20 requires the prescribed LTV to be maintained on an ongoing basis throughout the tenor. Under the revised framework, LTV is computed on the amount repayable at maturity, including interest, for bullet-repayment loans — not merely the amount disbursed.

On auctions: para 37 requires adequate notice to the borrower or legal heir, and one month from the public notice where the borrower cannot be located; para 38 requires advertisements in at least two newspapers, one regional and one national; para 39 puts the auction through the lender's trained employee or an empanelled auctioneer; para 40 sets a reserve price not less than 90% of current value, 85% only after two failed auctions; para 41 requires the first auction physically in the lending branch's district, an adjoining district or online auction only if the first fails; para 42 bars the lender and related parties from bidding; para 43 requires the value fetched and dues adjusted to go to the borrower, any surplus refunded within seven working days of receiving the full proceeds.

Why this rulebook matters today: the subjects reported as likely at the meeting sit directly on it — delinquencies in a book whose LTV is maintained ongoing (para 20) on collateral valued at the lower of a 30-day average and the preceding day's close (para 17), which is why a falling price reaches the books at once and why today's AGLOC ceiling is ₹439 a gram lower at the 85% band than the 20 Nov block. The industry's asks — Tier-I 12% to 10%, a review of the 100% risk weights, PSL inclusion — are representations, not rules; the industry has asked RBI to review, and no instrument doing any of them appears in the sources checked.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: these are the Directions Muthoot Fincorp's gold-loan book and auction practice run under; nothing in them changed today, and the reported delinquency discussion is a discussion, not an amendment.
