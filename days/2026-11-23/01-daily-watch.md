### RBI meets chief executives of top-tier NBFCs today; no agenda has been published
<!-- story: id=rbi-nbfc-ceo-meeting-2026-11 -->
<!-- tags: regulators=rbi; topics=policy,gold-loan,microfinance; category=regulation -->
The Reserve Bank of India has called chief executive officers of top-tier NBFCs to a meeting today, 23 November, as part of its periodic interaction with the central bank, the Economic Times reported on 20 November (01:32 IST). BusinessToday (21 Nov, updated 16:42 IST) and CNBC-TV18 (22 Nov, 22:10 IST) reported that executives from housing finance companies and microfinance institutions will also attend and that the Governor and all deputy governors are expected present; ET reported that Governor Sanjay Malhotra is most likely to chair, and that these meetings are an annual affair, the last in January 2026.

There is no fixed agenda. ET quotes a senior NBFC executive: "There is no specific agenda for the meeting so far but there could be a central point for the RBI to discuss like customer service was the last time." BusinessToday quotes an industry executive: "There is nothing specific on agenda, but it gives an opportunity to all of us to speak up and share insights and concerns."

The three reports expect cyber risks, higher delinquencies in gold loans, and the impact of a weak monsoon and drought in large agrarian states such as Maharashtra to come up. BusinessToday and CNBC-TV18 reported that MFIs may seek to replace the ₹3 lakh annual household income cap with state-linked thresholds, with the 50% repayment-to-income cap also in play. ET also carries TransUnionCibil figures it published earlier this month: gold-loan NPAs rose to 0.3% at end-August 2026 from 0.16% at end-March; 27% of gold-loan borrowers hold four or more gold loans; and outstanding gold loans reached a record ₹22.5 lakh crore in August 2026, almost three times August 2023.

No agenda has been published and the meeting has not happened at writing time: this report records it as reported and as pending, not as a rule change and not as a decision.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: no Muthoot Fincorp-specific detail about the meeting appears in the sources checked; the subjects reported as likely are sector-wide, and nothing is predicted here.

### The policy backdrop the meeting sits in: repo at 5.50%, MPC on 2–4 December, and the industry's asks
<!-- story: id=rbi-policy-backdrop-2026-11; continues=fgloc-gold-summit-kochi-2026 -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-fincorp,muthoot-finance; regulators=rbi; topics=policy,rates,funding,gold-loan; category=regulation -->
The meeting sits six days before the Monetary Policy Committee's next meeting, 2–4 December 2026, decision due 4 December. The policy repo rate stands at 5.50%, raised 25 basis points at the MPC meeting of 5–7 October 2026, with the stance changed to calibrated tightening (RBI home page; October resolution). October CPI inflation was 6.09%, up from 5.54% in September — the first breach of the 6% upper tolerance band in two years — and economists expect 25 basis points in December with a 6.00–6.25% terminal repo (Business Standard, 13 and 16 Nov).

ET reported on 23 Nov that bank executives met top RBI officials last week ahead of the 4 December review, with a 25 or 50 basis point hike being weighed; a person aware of the deliberations said stress is "building up in gold loans which will be felt more by lenders who gave loans close to 85% of loan to value and now need more collateral because gold prices have dropped sharply."

The industry's asks are representations, not changes: through the newly formed Federation of Gold Loan Companies, gold-loan NBFCs have asked RBI to review the minimum Tier-I capital requirement, seeking a cut from 12% to 10%, and the 100% risk weights on gold-loan exposures (Mint, 17 Nov); separately, IIFL Finance has called for gold loans by NBFCs to be brought back under priority-sector lending (Mint, 17 Nov). Muthoot Fincorp chief executive Shaji Varghese, quoted by Mint, questioned why gold-loan companies should face a higher capital threshold, and flagged the continuous LTV requirement and restrictions on rolling over consumer gold loans. No instrument doing any of this appears in the sources checked.
