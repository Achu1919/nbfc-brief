### The warnings became actions: legal auction notices are out, and lenders have told the RBI they are writing fresh loans at 60–65% LTV
<!-- story: id=gold-loan-margin-calls-2026-11 -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-finance,manappuram,iifl-finance; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,asset-quality,gold-price; category=asset-quality -->
Through Wednesday, this cycle's record was warnings and monitoring — the RBI's 23 November meeting with NBFC and HFC chief executives, the reported step-up in portfolio reporting, and Wednesday's first margin-call report. **Thursday is the day the warnings became actions, and three outlets reported it within 24 hours.** The Economic Times (Sangita Mehta, ET Bureau, last updated 26 November, 05:30 IST) reports that **gold finance companies have started issuing legal auction notices**, and — in ET's own sourcing, people with knowledge of the matter — **thresholds were breached in nearly 40% of gold-loan portfolios at such financiers, with the ratio crossing 95% in some cases**. The notices require borrowers to **repay part of the loan or pledge additional collateral**, failing which the pledged jewellery may be auctioned; **gold auctions are held 30 days after serving a legal notice**. ET states the mechanic plainly: **in the gold-loan business an LTV breach is deemed a default by the borrower even where no repayment obligation has been missed**, and margin calls are reaching a "sizable customer base" **for the first time in about four years**.

The number that matters most in Thursday's reporting is a representation, not a rule: **banks and gold-loan companies such as Muthoot and Manappuram have informed the RBI that, as a prudential measure, they have lowered the LTV on fresh loans to an average of 60–65% from about 75%** (ET's sources). **ET writes "Muthoot" without saying which entity, and this report keeps ET's wording exactly: Muthoot Finance is the listed company; Muthoot Fincorp is not named in the report, and nothing in it is attributed to Muthoot Fincorp.** The standing bands are unchanged — **85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh, 75% above ₹5 lakh** — and a representation to the RBI is not a rule change: what ET reports is lenders *informing* the regulator of where they are writing fresh business, written here as reported, not as a requirement.

The scale behind the notices is CRIF's, via ET: **the gold-loan portfolio expanded to about ₹23 lakh crore as of 30 September 2026, roughly 12% of the ₹185 lakh crore retail-loan market**. Two of ET's own market citations need their labels stated: its IBJA reference — 999 at **₹1,21,038 per 10 g on a rolling one-month basis, against evening closing rates of ₹1,41,710 on 26 October 2026** — is **IBJA's 25 November PM print, not a 26 November figure**; IBJA's 26 November PM 999 is ₹1,22,641, the benchmark used below. And ET's "$3,481" international price, via investing.com, is an **intraday reference print, not a Comex settlement** — the last settlement on the record is 25 November's $3,534.70 — and stays out of the settlement record.

BusinessLine (Ashokamithran T, updated 26 November, 20:41 IST) reports the same turn from inside the lenders: **gold-loan companies are shifting focus from growth to collections** after the price fall pushed LTVs higher. Industry executives put the arithmetic in numbers: **portfolio LTVs at some gold-loan-focused NBFCs have crossed 75%, and certain segments may have breached 85%**; **loans originated at 80–85% LTV after the RBI relaxed norms for lower-ticket loans have jumped to 90–95%** after the correction. A senior executive at a leading gold-loan company, unnamed, is quoted directly: **"My full effort right now is into collections. Growth is not happening. In November, I am not focused on growth at all. The focus is reducing the LTV and collections."** Companies have started offering **interest-rate rebates and other incentives** to keep borrowers servicing. **Parth Gutka of 360 ONE Capital**: "Loans that originated at over 60 per cent LTV have now reached 75 per cent and this would roughly be half of the overall gold loan portfolio" — NBFCs more vulnerable than banks, he adds. **Piran Engineer, Senior Research Analyst at CLSA India**, expects stress to start surfacing **as early as the December quarter**, with lenders demanding additional collateral and repayments; executives see fresh business taking a back seat until collateral values stabilise.

Business Today (Amit Mudgill, 26 November, 10:36 IST) carries Antique Stock Broking's scenario analysis, in Antique's own words: **"Gold prices have corrected 30 per cent from their January 2026 peak, and our analysis suggests that loans disbursed in 1QFY27 have nearly breached the regulatory LTV limit at current prices. A further 21 per cent correction in gold prices across the loan tenure could result in recovery losses on slippages from the 1QFY27 disbursement vintage."** Antique calls that further decline "very steep" and **limits the risk to loans with tenures exceeding six months**. It names the structure now showing up in the books: **the shift from bullet repayments to monthly EMIs is contributing to higher Stage 2/3 assets in Q2FY27**, a near-term headwind expected to normalise over the next couple of quarters. The historical comparison is FY21, when gold corrected 15% between August 2020 and February 2021: **recovery rates were 84% for Muthoot, 95% for Manappuram and 99% for IIFL Finance, with no major credit-cost spike**. **"Muthoot" in Antique's series is Muthoot Finance, the listed company — not Muthoot Fincorp.** Antique's ratings, reported as the brokerage's view: **Manappuram 'Buy', target cut to ₹370; Muthoot Finance 'Hold', target ₹2,650** — it prefers Manappuram. Those are calls on two listed peers, not on this company.

The three correction figures in circulation on 26 November do not agree, and this report does not blend them: **ET puts the fall at 35% from the January peak, and 11% in November so far; Antique puts it at 30% from the January 2026 peak; BusinessLine puts it at nearly 19% from their January peaks.** They measure different reference points and are not reconcilable from the sources in this pack. Each figure carries its outlet's name; none is picked and none is blended.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the mechanism ET, BusinessLine and Antique describe — legal notices, part-repayment, additional collateral, auction 30 days after a notice, an LTV breach deemed a default without a missed repayment — is the ongoing-LTV maintenance that applies across gold-loan lenders, including Muthoot Fincorp, and the collections-over-growth shift is described by industry executives, not by any named company. The collateral arithmetic runs one line at a time and in both directions: **the LTV bands are fixed percentages of a reference price, so when the reference rises ₹146, the permissible loan value per gram rises with it — ₹124 at the 85% band, ₹118 at 80%, ₹110 at 75% — on every ticket size at once.** The same mechanism ran downward through 23 and 25 November and upward today; no trend is extrapolated from either.
