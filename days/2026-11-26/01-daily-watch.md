### Margin calls have started: banks and NBFCs are asking gold-loan borrowers for more collateral or part-repayment
<!-- story: id=gold-loan-margin-calls-2026-11 -->
<!-- tags: companies=iifl-finance; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,asset-quality,gold-price; category=asset-quality -->
The report that changes the shape of this story landed in Mint on 25 November at 11:55 IST: "Sharp drop in gold prices triggers margin calls on loans by banks and NBFCs — what customers should know about LTV", by Bobins Vayalil Abraham. **Banks and NBFCs have accelerated sending notices and reminders to customers, asking them to provide additional security or repay a part of their gold loan, because the pledged gold no longer covers the RBI LTV limit.** Everything before this in the cycle was warning and monitoring — the RBI's 23 November meeting with NBFC, HFC and microfinance chief executives in Mumbai (readout, press release 2026-2027/1554), the reported increase in portfolio-reporting frequency, the caution that LTVs skew as prices fall. **This is the first report in this cycle that borrowers are actually being asked for more collateral, not merely warned.**

The credit data behind the asking is TransUnion CIBIL's, as cited in the same piece: **gold-loan NPAs stood at 0.30% at end-August 2026, up from 0.16% at 31 March 2026**, and borrowers with multiple gold-loan accounts are more prone to delay. The piece's own market read puts 24K gold hovering between ₹12,458 and ₹12,632 a gram on 25 November, with prices fallen roughly 30% to 35% from their January 2026 record highs — Mint's quote of the retail market, not an IBJA print, and kept apart from the benchmark figures below.

Two named industry voices carry the lender side, each attributed to the person and the outlet. **Mayank Sharma, president-lending at IIFL Finance, told PTI** the industry "could see a significant increase in gold auctions this year once prices stabilise over a longer period", and: "At the current gold price, there will be a greater focus on customer collections and interest collections." **Pinaki Nath Banerjee, CEO-designate of the Federation of Gold Loan Companies (FGLOC)**, said repayment challenges have emerged after the correction — and that fresh loan demand remains healthy; the industry is unlikely to match its prior growth, because rising prices had inflated collateral values. IIFL Finance is a listed gold-loan peer and FGLOC is the industry's federation; **neither voice speaks for Muthoot Fincorp**, and the CIBIL figure is an industry-level metric, not any single lender's book. Muthoot Finance is a separate listed company, and no statement in this report belongs to it unless attributed to it.

The piece restates the RBI's standing LTV bands — **85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh, 75% above ₹5 lakh** — the structure under which such notices go out. No RBI instrument dated 24, 25 or 26 November changes them, and the rules report today records that no instrument of any regulator changes a rule applying to Muthoot Fincorp's lending business.

What was already on the record, each item with its own date and none of it today's news: the Economic Times reported on 24 November at 01:11 IST that the RBI told NBFCs at the 23 November meeting to scrutinise gold-loan LTVs as prices fall, and that the ₹10-crore net owned fund threshold arrives by March 2027 with no further extension; Moneycontrol reported on 23 November at 10:04 IST that the RBI has raised the frequency of portfolio reporting over the last 30–45 days, that some loans stand above 100% LTV, and that gold loans stood at **₹3.74 trillion in September 2026, up 66.1% year-on-year**; and Crisil, via Business Today on 24 November, expects gold-loan AUM growth to moderate to about **40% in FY27 from 78% in FY26** and does not anticipate a significant rise in credit costs.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the mechanism Mint describes — notices, additional security, part-repayment, auctions — is the same ongoing-LTV maintenance that applies across gold-loan lenders, including Muthoot Fincorp. No public document states how many such notices the company has sent or where its book sits against the bands, and no such figure is asserted here.

SEBI's two 25 November circulars are new since yesterday's edition, and both are real instruments; **neither is addressed to an NBFC as a lender.** The half-yearly review of 'Significant Indices' under the SEBI (Index Providers) Regulations, 2024 — circular HO/47/17/12(53)2026-MRD-POD5/I/26407/2026 — reviewed the six months to 30 June 2026 and **leaves the Annexure-A list unchanged**: BSE 200 and Nifty CPSE, which did not meet the ₹20,000-crore AUM-tracking threshold, are retained under Para 5 of the May 2026 circular. The second — the simplification of client on-boarding and rationalisation of risk management at KYC Registration Agencies, HO/38/14/(25)2026-MIRSD-POD3 — amends the October 2023 KYC Master Circular: it centralises supplementary client information at KRA level, adds CKYC ID, DIGIPIN and OVD expiry date to the upload set, requires intermediaries to upload within three working days of completing KYC, and **takes effect 24 December 2026**. Its scope is **intermediaries registered under Section 12 of the SEBI Act, recognised stock exchanges, clearing corporations and depositories**. **A gold-loan NBFC is not a Section 12 intermediary unless separately registered — for example as a depository participant — and this circular does not bind Muthoot Fincorp's lending business**; its relevance runs to group or affiliate entities that are SEBI-registered intermediaries, and to KYC data quality generally. IBBI was checked with no update date assertable from the page as read.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: none today. The operative rulebook — the RBI's gold-loan LTV framework — is unchanged, and both circulars' scopes stop short of NBFC lending.

### What a fresh reference does: the ceiling falls with the price, and the notices follow
<!-- story: id=gold-price-weekly; dup -->
<!-- tags: topics=gold-loan,regulation; category=gold -->
The collateral arithmetic is one line: **the LTV bands are fixed percentages of a reference price, so when the reference falls 3.13% in one print, the permissible loan value per gram falls with it — ₹304 at the 85% band, ₹287 at 80%, ₹268 at 75% — on every ticket size at once.** A loan written against a higher reference then sits above its band until the borrower adds security or repays part of it, which is exactly what Mint reported lenders asking for on 25 November, under the RBI's standing bands — 85% up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh, 75% above ₹5 lakh.

The credit-side background is on the record with its dates and its companies, each figure the company's own: Moneycontrol on 23 November at 10:04 IST reported **Bajaj Finance's Stage 2 gold loans at 4.51%, from 0.65%**, and **Muthoot Finance's Stage 2 at ₹23,466 crore against ₹534 crore a year earlier** — Bajaj Finance's number belongs to Bajaj Finance and Muthoot Finance's to Muthoot Finance, a listed peer, not Muthoot Fincorp; neither figure is this company's.
