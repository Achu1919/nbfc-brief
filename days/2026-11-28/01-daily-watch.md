### Friday's rally does not fix the LTV arithmetic — the books were written near the peak, and one up-session does not re-write them
<!-- story: id=gold-loan-margin-calls-2026-11 -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-finance,manappuram,iifl-finance; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,asset-quality,gold-price; category=asset-quality -->
The reporting published across Thursday and Friday measures the loan books against the collateral fall, and none of it says the fall has been worked off. **The Economic Times (26 November, 05:30 IST) reports that thresholds were breached in nearly 40% of gold-loan portfolios, with the ratio crossing 95% in some cases**, that gold finance companies have started issuing legal auction notices, and that **gold auctions are held 30 days after serving a legal notice**. In the gold-loan business an LTV breach is deemed a default even where no repayment obligation has been missed — the standing maintenance rule, carried with its bands in the regulation report. ET also reports, in its own sourcing, that **banks and lenders have told the RBI they have lowered the LTV on fresh loans to an average of 60–65% from about 75%** — a representation to the regulator, written here exactly as reported: the RBI has not relaxed or cut anything, and where fresh loans are being written below the bands is prudential discretion, not a requirement on any named company.

The arithmetic of the fall, as published. **Moneycontrol (27 November, 17:15 IST) reports that a loan written at 70% LTV near the peak is now worth about 103% of its collateral, and that bullet-repayment loans carry the most risk** — on Moneycontrol's own two figures, the collateral behind such a loan has fallen to roughly two-thirds of the price it was written against. Moneycontrol also reports bank gold-loan growth of 83% year-on-year in October — the growth that wrote the book now being managed down. **BusinessLine (26 November, 20:41 IST) reports that portfolio LTVs at some gold-loan NBFCs have crossed 75%, with certain segments possibly having breached 85%, and loans originated at 80–85% LTV now standing at 90–95%**, the lenders shifting into collection mode rather than growth mode. **The asymmetry is arithmetic, not opinion: Friday's 2.98% PM-to-PM rise in the 916 rate recovers a small part of a fall of that size, not the fall itself — and AGLOC's new block, below, re-strikes the reference to Friday's close for the 28-30 November window; it is the same price applied forward, not new collateral value.** Moneycontrol's 103% figure was published Friday evening, 17:15 IST, and the pack does not state which session's prices it measures; it is carried as Moneycontrol's, not restated as any book's position.

Antique Stock Broking's view, as reported by Livemint on 27 November and attributed here as a brokerage view: **the 1QFY27 disbursement vintage has nearly breached the regulatory LTV limit at current prices, and a further 21% correction in gold prices across the loan tenure could cause recovery losses on that vintage.** Antique's ratings in the same report — **'Buy' on Manappuram with the target cut to ₹370, 'Hold' on Muthoot Finance at ₹2,650** — are calls on two listed peers, not on this company.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the ongoing-LTV maintenance the reporting describes applies across gold-loan lenders, including Muthoot Fincorp.
