## 🔔 New Regulatory Updates Today

Three RBI instruments were issued on 23 November — parallel amendments to the small finance bank, NBFC and AIFI securitisation directions — and **one of them binds Muthoot Fincorp**: the Non-Banking Financial Companies (Securitisation Transactions) Amendment Directions, 2026, effective 1 April 2027. Nothing else in the RBI, SEBI or IBBI lists changes a rule that applies to the company. The day's substantive regulatory event is not an instrument but a meeting: the RBI met the chief executives of select NBFCs, HFCs and microfinance institutions on Monday, and the official readout is now out, with reporting that it asked lenders for greater scrutiny of gold-loan LTVs. What was checked is set out below; the substance is the standing gold-loan rulebook, that meeting, and the next scheduled decision, the MPC on 2–4 December.

### The standing rulebook: RBI (Lending Against Gold and Silver Collateral) Directions, 2025
<!-- reg: ref=RBI/2025-26/47 DOR.CRE.REC.26/21.01.023/2025-26; regulator=RBI; issued=2025-06-06; effective=2026-04-01; status=in-force; lifecycle=effective; applies=nbfcs -->
<!-- story: id=reg-rbi-rbi-2025-26-47 -->
The Directions were issued on 6 June 2025 (RBI/2025-26/47 DOR.CRE.REC.26/21.01.023/2025-26), updated on 29 September 2025 by the (1st Amendment) Directions (RBI/2025-26/84 DOR.CRE.REC.52/21.01.023/2025-26); compliance was required by 1 April 2026, and loans sanctioned before a lender adopted them stay under the earlier rules. Para 15 caps bullet-repayment consumption loans at 12 months, renewable under para 11; para 16 caps ornaments at 1 kg of gold (10 kg silver) and coins at 50 g gold (500 g silver) per borrower; para 17 values collateral at the lower of the average closing price of that purity over the preceding 30 days and the closing price on the preceding day, per IBJA or a SEBI-regulated commodity exchange; para 19 sets maximum LTV at 85% (loans up to ₹2.5 lakh), 80% (₹2.5–5 lakh) and 75% (above ₹5 lakh); para 20 requires the prescribed LTV to be maintained on an ongoing basis throughout the tenor. LTV is computed on the amount repayable at maturity, including interest, for bullet-repayment loans — not merely the amount disbursed.

On auctions: para 37 requires adequate notice to the borrower or legal heir, and one month from the public notice where the borrower cannot be located; para 38 requires advertisements in at least two newspapers, one regional and one national; para 39 puts the auction through the lender's trained employee or an empanelled auctioneer; para 40 sets a reserve price not less than 90% of current value, 85% only after two failed auctions; para 41 requires the first auction physically in the lending branch's district, an adjoining district or online auction only if the first fails; para 42 bars the lender and related parties from bidding; para 43 requires the value fetched and dues adjusted to go to the borrower, any surplus refunded within seven working days of receiving the full proceeds.

Why this rulebook matters this week: every subject reported out of Monday's meeting sits on it. The continuous-LTV requirement in para 20, applied to collateral valued under para 17 at the lower of a 30-day average and the preceding day's close, is the mechanism that turns a price fall into reported LTV breaches with a lag — the average is still carrying pre-fall prints while spot has already dropped. Today's AGLOC ceiling is ₹9,728 a gram at the 85% band, up ₹82 from Monday's ₹9,646 but still far below where the collateral stood when most of this year's book was written. The industry's asks — a lower Tier-1 requirement, a revisit of the LTV rules and priority-sector inclusion — are representations; the industry has asked the RBI to review, and no instrument doing any of them appears in the sources checked.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: these are the Directions its gold-loan book and auction practice run under; nothing in them changed today, and the reported scrutiny is a discussion, not an amendment.

### The new instruments, and who they actually bind: three parallel securitisation amendments
<!-- reg: ref=RBI/2026-27/306 DOR.STR.REC.258/21-04-177/2026-27; regulator=RBI; issued=2026-11-23; effective=2027-04-01; status=new; lifecycle=final; applies=nbfcs -->
<!-- story: id=reg-rbi-rbi-2026-27-306 -->
The RBI issued **three parallel securitisation amendments on 23 November 2026**, one for each class of lender, all effective **1 April 2027**:

| Instrument | Reference | Notification | Binds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Finance Banks — Securitisation Transactions | RBI/2026-27/305, DOR.STR.REC.257/21-04-177/2026-27 | Id=13754 | Small finance banks |
| **Non-Banking Financial Companies — Securitisation Transactions** | **RBI/2026-27/306, DOR.STR.REC.258/21-04-177/2026-27** | **Id=13755** | **NBFCs, including Muthoot Fincorp** |
| All India Financial Institutions — Securitisation Transactions | RBI/2026-27/307, DOR.STR.REC.259/21-04-177/2026-27 | Id=13756 | NABARD, NHB, SIDBI, EXIM Bank, NaBFID |

In each, para 26 is substituted so that the issuance, holding and subsequent transfer of securitisation notes shall only be in **dematerialised form**, with a **minimum ticket size of ₹1 crore** at the time of issuance and at any subsequent transfer, and the originator must ensure its agreement with the special purpose entity requires arrangements with the registrar, depository and stock exchange to hold that floor. A proviso allows notes with amortisation structures to be traded at amortised value if the ticket size falls below ₹1 crore. A new para 27A requires transactions in notes issued before 1 April 2027 to comply as well.

**Scope is the point, and it cuts the other way from a single instrument.** The NBFC amendment modifies the NBFC Securitisation Transactions Directions, 2025 — the framework under which Muthoot Fincorp runs its direct assignments and co-lending. Its Q2 FY27 filing records ₹8,137.9 crore of direct assignment in the quarter (1,65,596 accounts) and co-lending outstanding of ₹3,583.8 crore across ten arrangements. The AIFI directions bind only the all-India institutions, and the SFB directions bind small finance banks; neither reaches the company. The National Housing Bank's presence at Monday's meeting is a separate fact and does not change any of these scopes.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: no effect on LTV, credit or provisioning rules, and no cost today. From 1 April 2027 any securitisation note the company issues or holds must be dematerialised with a ₹1 crore minimum ticket — a treasury and struct-ops item to diarise, and a question for the transaction documents on existing deals rather than for this edition.

### The day's regulatory event: the RBI's NBFC/HFC meeting, and the gold-loan scrutiny reported out of it
<!-- story: id=rbi-nbfc-ceo-meeting-2026-11; dup -->
The RBI met the managing directors and chief executives of select NBFCs, including government NBFCs, HFCs and microfinance institutions, in Mumbai on 23 November, per its own press release 2026-2027/1554 signed by Brij Raj, Chief General Manager. Representatives of Sa-Dhan, MFIN and FIDC participated; all four Deputy Governors attended with other senior officers; the MD and CEO of the National Housing Bank also participated. The release itself records the Governor acknowledging the role of NBFCs and HFCs in supporting credit flow and is a readout, not an instrument — no rule changed. Reporting on the meeting adds that the RBI asked for greater scrutiny of gold loans as falling prices pressure LTVs (Economic Times ed. 24 Nov, 01:11 IST), that it has raised the frequency of portfolio reporting over the last 30–45 days (Moneycontrol, 23 Nov), and that NBFCs failing to reach a ₹10 crore net owned fund by March 2027 will get no further extension (ET). Those are reported, not gazetted: the net-owned-fund deadline already exists in the scale-based framework.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the scrutiny is sector-wide; its own Stage III is 1.07% at 30 September 2026, a lagging indicator against a fall that mostly happened after that date. No supervisory action against any named lender is reported.

### The next scheduled decision: the MPC meets 2–4 December
<!-- story: id=rbi-policy-backdrop-2026-11 -->
The Monetary Policy Committee meets on 2–4 December 2026, with the decision due on **4 December**. The policy repo rate stands at **5.50%**, raised 25 basis points at the 5–7 October meeting, with the stance changed to calibrated tightening; SDF 5.25%, MSF and Bank Rate 5.75%. October CPI inflation was **6.09%**, the first breach of the 6% upper tolerance band in two years, and economists expect a further 25 basis points in December with a terminal repo of 6.00–6.25%. ET reported on 23 November that the RBI is weighing a 25 or 50 basis point hike and that bankers raised gold-loan stress in the pre-policy consultations. This is inside the MPC's pre-policy quiet period, when no member comments publicly on the decision before it is announced.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the repo sets the floor under its cost of funds, and the last published average cost of borrowing was 9.11% for FY26. A 25 basis point hike is not itself the risk; the risk is the pass-through into bank term loans, which were 39.69% of the borrowing mix.
