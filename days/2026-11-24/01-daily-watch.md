### RBI presses NBFCs on gold-loan LTVs as collateral falls, ET reports
<!-- story: id=rbi-nbfc-ceo-meeting-2026-11 -->
<!-- tags: regulators=rbi; topics=policy,gold-loan,governance; category=regulation -->
The Reserve Bank of India met the managing directors and chief executives of select NBFCs, including government NBFCs, housing finance companies and microfinance institutions, in Mumbai on Monday 23 November, and the official readout is now out. RBI press release 2026-2027/1554, dated 23 November and signed by Brij Raj, Chief General Manager, records that representatives of Sa-Dhan, MFIN and FIDC also participated, that **all four Deputy Governors — Shri Swaminathan J., Dr Poonam Gupta, Shri S. C. Murmu and Shri Rohit Jain** — attended with other senior officers, and that the **Managing Director and Chief Executive of the National Housing Bank also participated**.

The part that matters to a gold lender is not in the release. The Economic Times reported on Tuesday 24 November (01:11 IST) that the RBI "told non-banking finance companies about the need for greater scrutiny in their gold loans given falling gold prices that skew unfavourably for lenders' loan to value ratios", adding that gold has fallen close to 30% from its peak "in the last month or so, which means that outstanding value for some loans may have shrunk below the 85% loan to value ratio that the central bank stipulates for loans against gold."

ET quotes a person aware of the discussions that the meeting ran one and a half hours, and that "with gold prices down 35% to 40% and some of the companies close to 75% in terms of loan to value … The RBI said that companies must be more cautious. NBFCs were told that growth has been good so far but were cautioned against growing too quickly." The same report says NBFCs that fail to reach a ₹10 crore net owned fund by March 2027 will get no further extension, and that the RBI wanted greater scrutiny when onboarding new technology partners.

Moneycontrol reported the monitoring side on Monday (23 Nov, 10:04 IST): the RBI "has started increasing its portfolio monitoring over the last 30–45 days" and "has been asking all large NBFCs to report their portfolio numbers at a higher frequency compared to a monthly assessment." A senior executive at a gold loan company, speaking on condition of anonymity, said that "around 18 months back, when gold prices were at their all-time high, LTV was comfortably within the 70-80 percent level. Now with the steep correction that has happened, there are certain cases where LTV is seen breaching even 100 percent."

Two numbers put the scale on it. TransUnion CIBIL, cited by ET, has gold-loan NPAs at **0.30% at the end of August 2026 against 0.16% at 31 March 2026**. Moneycontrol, citing RBI data, puts loans against gold at **₹3.74 trillion in September 2026, up 66.1% year-on-year**.

The industry's response is a representation, not a change: the newly formed Federation of Gold Loan Companies, whose members include Muthoot Finance and Manappuram Finance, has asked the RBI to review the Tier-1 capital requirement so it aligns with other NBFCs, and to bring gold loans back into priority-sector lending.

Nothing here is a rule change. The RBI released no instrument on gold-loan LTV; the 85%/80%/75% ceilings and the 30-day-average valuation rule already stand in the June 2025 Directions. No lender has been named as breaching, and no company has been named as under supervisory action.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the reported scrutiny is sector-wide and no Muthoot Fincorp-specific detail appears in the sources checked. Its own last published asset-quality number is Stage III at 1.07% as at 30 September 2026, filed on 14 November, which is a lagging indicator against a price fall that mostly happened in October and November. The operative sentence for planning is the one about continuous LTV: the June 2025 Directions require the prescribed LTV to be maintained throughout the tenor, and the collateral is valued at the lower of a 30-day average and the previous day's close, which is why a fall reaches the book with a lag rather than on the day it happens.

### What the 30-day-average rule does to a book after a 30% fall and a 3.9% bounce
<!-- story: id=gold-price-weekly; dup -->
<!-- tags: topics=gold-price,gold-loan,regulation; category=gold -->
The June 2025 Directions require collateral to be valued at the **lower of the average closing price of that purity over the preceding 30 days and the closing price on the preceding day**, and the prescribed LTV to be maintained **throughout the tenor**. That combination is why the reported LTV breaches appear after a price fall rather than on the day of it: the average still carries the pre-fall prints while spot has already dropped, so the permitted loan value comes down gradually and a loan written at the old ceiling drifts above the current one without any new lending.

Worked on IBJA's own 916 series, and this is an illustration rather than any lender's book: 916 fell from **₹1,34,201 per 10 g on 17 November's PM session to ₹1,13,486 on 20 November's PM session**, a fall of **15.4%** in three sessions. A loan written at the 75% ceiling against the 17 November reference would sit at about **88.7%** of the 20 November reference on the same collateral — above the 85% ceiling that applies to any ticket size. The division is ₹1,34,201 ÷ ₹1,13,486 = 1.1825, and 75% × 1.1825 = 88.7%.

Monday's move runs the same arithmetic the other way but too slowly to matter yet: 916 at **₹1,14,450 on 23 November's PM session is up 0.85%** on 20 November, and Comex gold rose 3.90% on the day. One session does not repair a 30-day average that is still rolling over the past three weeks' lower prints. Two or three more sessions in the same direction would start to. **The 30-day average is the number to watch this week, not the spot print.**

What would change the picture, stated as conditions rather than predictions: a further fall in spot; the MPC's decision on 4 December; or any RBI communication that turns Monday's reported "greater scrutiny" into a written instruction. None of those has happened.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: this is the mechanism its book runs on, and it is direction-neutral — it dampens the damage from a fall and equally dampens the relief from a bounce. No Muthoot Fincorp book is described here.

### Three parallel securitisation amendments — and one of them binds Muthoot Fincorp
<!-- story: id=lenders-other-circulars; dup -->
<!-- tags: regulators=rbi; topics=regulation,securitisation; category=regulation -->
The RBI issued **three parallel securitisation amendments on 23 November**, one for each class of lender, all effective **1 April 2027**: the Small Finance Banks directions (RBI/2026-27/305, DOR.STR.REC.257/21-04-177/2026-27), the **Non-Banking Financial Companies directions (RBI/2026-27/306, DOR.STR.REC.258/21-04-177/2026-27)** and the All India Financial Institutions directions (RBI/2026-27/307, DOR.STR.REC.259/21-04-177/2026-27), the last signed by Vaibhav Chaturvedi, Chief General Manager.

In each, para 26 is substituted so that the issuance, holding and transfer of securitisation notes is **only in dematerialised form**, with a **minimum ticket size of ₹1 crore** at issuance and at every subsequent transfer; a new para 27A extends the requirement to notes issued before 1 April 2027. A proviso allows notes with amortisation structures to be traded at amortised value if the ticket falls below ₹1 crore.

**The NBFC one is the one that matters here.** It amends the NBFC Securitisation Transactions Directions, 2025, which govern the assignments and co-lending arrangements Muthoot Fincorp actually uses — the Q2 filing records ₹8,137.9 crore of direct assignment in the quarter and ₹3,583.8 crore of co-lending outstanding. The AIFI directions bind NABARD, NHB, SIDBI, EXIM Bank and NaBFID and do not bind the company; the SFB directions bind small finance banks. The National Housing Bank's presence at Monday's meeting is a separate fact and does not change any of these scopes.

SEBI issued nothing new: its circulars listing, re-fetched this morning, still has the **17 November 2026** circular on the commodity derivatives margin framework as its newest entry, with nothing dated 18 to 24 November.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: not a cost today, and not a change to LTV or credit rules. The dematerialised-notes and ₹1 crore ticket floor change the plumbing of any securitisation the company does or buys from 1 April 2027, which is a treasury and struct-ops item to diarise rather than a Q3 issue. The effect on its existing assignments is a question for the transaction documents, not for this edition.

## 🏘️ Sector Deep Dive — Tuesday: why a 15% fall in three sessions reads as a 100% LTV

### The lag is the story, and it runs both ways
<!-- tags: topics=gold-loan,regulation; category=analysis -->
The number that circulated on Monday — that certain cases are "breaching even 100 percent" LTV — is not a claim that lenders lent at 100%. It is what a constant-LTV rule produces when the collateral is marked on a trailing average and the price moves fast in one direction.

Take a book written near the January peak, when the 24-carat price was above ₹1.8 lakh per 10 g. A loan at 75% of collateral value then is at roughly 75% × (₹18,000 ÷ ₹12,495) ≈ 108% of the current 999 reference if nothing else changed — before any allowance for the 30-day average, which softens it slightly but does not rescue it. That cohort exists in the industry, and it is the cohort behind the reported breaches.

The other side of the same mechanism is the one to watch now. Because the average lags, a 3.9% bounce does not immediately restore lending headroom, and a further 5% fall does not immediately remove it. **The average, not the spot, sets the ceiling** — which means the next two weeks of prints matter more than any single session, and the 4 December MPC matters more than either, because it sets the cost of the funding that carries the book through the lag.

Crisil's read, published on 23 November, is that gold loans stay the fastest-growing NBFC segment at a roughly 40% CAGR over this fiscal and next after 78% growth last fiscal, that NBFC AUM reaches about ₹68 lakh crore by March 2028, and that a steep decline of more than 15% in gold prices is rare and unlikely to trigger a significant rise in credit costs. That is a rating agency's view of the sector and it is reported as such.
Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the vintage of its own book — how much was written in January and February near the peak — is the single number that determines whether this is a manageable tail or a provisioning event, and it is not in any public document.
