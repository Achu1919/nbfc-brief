### Gold's worst week since March, and the lower lending ceiling that applies from Monday
<!-- story: id=gold-price-weekly -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-finance,manappuram,iifl-finance; topics=gold-price,gold-loan,markets; category=gold -->
Gold has just had its worst week since March. Front-month Comex gold ended Friday at $3,512.30 an ounce, down $367.40, or 9.47%, for the week — in the Dow Jones Data Talk carried by Morningstar (20 Nov, 18:53 ET), the "largest one week net and percentage decline since the week ending March 20, 2026". The Comex December contract settled at $3,515.40, −4.62%, and December silver at $41.07, −7.41%, the steepest fall on the board (The Edge's official-settlement wrap); the front-month figure and the December settlement are different contracts.

Domestically, IBJA's 999 rate for the 20 Nov PM session was ₹1,23,893 per 10 g (₹12,389.3 a gram), ₹5,638 per 10 g (−4.35%) below the 19 Nov PM session; the 916 rate was ₹1,13,486 per 10 g (₹11,348.6 a gram). From Tuesday 17 Nov's PM session, the earliest row IBJA still shows, 999 is down ₹10,308 per 10 g (−7.68%) — three sessions of change (18, 19 and 20 Nov), not a full week. There is no 21 Nov rate: IBJA states it does not publish on central government holidays or on Saturdays and Sundays, so the 20 Nov PM session is the latest published benchmark.

AGLOC's next block applies from Monday 23 November: maximum permissible loan-to-value ₹9,646 a gram at 85% — ₹439 below the 20 Nov block — with ₹9,078 at 80% (−₹414) and ₹8,511 at 75% (−₹387), on a jewellery reference of ₹11,348, itself ₹517 lower and equal to IBJA's 20 Nov PM 916 rate rounded down. The ceiling is a rupee figure per gram, so a lower reference lowers what the same pledged gold supports: fresh loans against the same grams are smaller, and existing loans written close to a ceiling carry a thinner cushion.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the ceiling that applies from Monday is ₹439 a gram lower than Friday's, so each gram pledged at the 85% band supports a smaller maximum loan and existing loans near a ceiling carry a thinner cushion. The sources checked give no Muthoot Fincorp LTV or loan-book figure for 20 or 21 Nov.

### Motilal Oswal keeps Neutral on the gold financiers and expects elevated Stage 2
<!-- story: id=gold-loan-asset-quality-2026 -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-finance,iifl-finance,bajaj-finance,manappuram; topics=asset-quality,gold-loan; category=asset-quality -->
What does the fall do to the book? Motilal Oswal's answer, as reported by the Financial Express (20 Nov, 15:23 IST): the September-quarter results of Muthoot Finance, IIFL Finance, Bajaj Finance and Manappuram Finance show "a broad-based increase in stage 2 gold loan assets", attributed "predominantly" to customers adjusting to the revised repayment architecture, "particularly the introduction of monthly interest servicing".

The brokerage keeps a Neutral rating on the gold financiers under its coverage — mainly Muthoot Finance and Manappuram — citing rising competition, the need to adapt to regulation and gold-price volatility. It expects elevated Stage 2 "over the next couple of quarters, with some migration into stage 3", and describes a transition to "structurally lower profitability".

Every line above is the brokerage's view — not company guidance, and not a finding about any company's book.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: the brokerage's read covers the listed peer set, not Muthoot Fincorp. The sources checked give no Muthoot Fincorp Stage 2 figure; the latest published number is Stage III at 1.07% in the 14 Nov Q2 filing, against 1.41% a year earlier.

### Pledged tonnage rises at the three large lenders while its value falls
<!-- story: id=gold-pledged-tonnage-2026-11-21 -->
<!-- tags: companies=muthoot-finance,manappuram,iifl-finance; topics=gold-loan,results; category=gold -->
Moneycontrol's 20 Nov report (10:15 IST) puts pledged tonnage up at all three large lenders in Q2 FY27: Muthoot Finance held 204 tonnes of gold as security, against 197 tonnes in Q1 FY27; Manappuram Finance 70 tonnes, against 67 in Q1 and 57 in Q2 FY26; and IIFL Finance 69.3 metric tonnes, against 64.3 MT in Q1. The sources checked give no comparable Muthoot Fincorp tonnage figure.

The mechanism, in the words of Pinaki Nath Banerjee, CEO-designate of the Federation of Gold Loan Companies: "When prices correct, borrowers may have to pledge a larger quantity of gold to raise the same amount of money. Therefore, the tonnage of gold pledged may increase even when prices have moderated."

The market-level numbers point the same way. CRIF High Mark's Q2 FY27 data, reported by CNBC-TV18 on 18 Nov, show originations rising 27.2% by value to ₹8.15 lakh crore on accounts falling 4.1% to 369.4 lakh, with NBFCs' share by value at 31.4%, up about 4 percentage points quarter on quarter, in a market estimated at ₹23.3 lakh crore, up more than 60% over the past year. More rupees against fewer accounts is growth from the collateral's price, not from more borrowers. Moneycontrol also reported, without citing a circular, that the RBI "started to monitor bullion portfolios of banks and NBFCs" as gold cooled from its January highs — a media report, not a rule change.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: none stated in the sources checked, which give no Muthoot Fincorp pledged-tonnage, LTV or auction figure for November.
Sources: [Moneycontrol, 20 Nov](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/gold-loan-companies-record-increase-in-bullion-tonnage-in-q2-as-bullion-prices-cool-off-14085426.html) · [CNBC-TV18, 18 Nov](https://www.cnbctv18.com/business/finance/explained-why-gold-price-today-may-hit-gold-loan-firms-20036646.htm)
