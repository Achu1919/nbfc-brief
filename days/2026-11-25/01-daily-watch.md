### What the 30-day-average rule does with a two-session round trip
<!-- story: id=gold-price-weekly; dup -->
<!-- tags: topics=gold-price,gold-loan,regulation; category=gold -->
The June 2025 Directions value collateral at the **lower of the average closing price of that purity over the preceding 30 days and the closing price on the preceding day** (para 17), and require the prescribed LTV to be maintained **on an ongoing basis throughout the tenor** (para 20). Monday's 3.90% bounce and Tuesday's 2.45% give-back therefore cancel at the spot level within two sessions, while the 30-day average keeps rolling — and the average, not either print, is the number that governs the ceiling. **A two-session round trip changes nothing in the arithmetic.**

Worked on IBJA's own 916 series, and labelled an illustration rather than any lender's book: 916 went from **₹1,34,201 per 10 g on 17 November's PM session to ₹1,13,486 on 20 November's PM session, a fall of 15.4% in three sessions**. A loan written at the 75% ceiling against the 17 November reference would sit at about **88.7% of the 20 November reference on the same collateral — above the 85% ceiling that applies to any ticket size**. The division is ₹1,34,201 ÷ ₹1,13,486 = 1.1825, and 75% × 1.1825 = 88.7%.

The background to that arithmetic is already on the record, each item with its own date, and none of it is today's news: the RBI met NBFC, HFC and microfinance chief executives on 23 November (readout, press release 2026-2027/1554); the Economic Times reported on 24 November at 01:11 IST that the RBI asked for greater scrutiny of gold-loan LTVs as prices fall, citing TransUnion CIBIL's gold-loan NPAs at **0.30% at end-August 2026 against 0.16% at 31 March 2026**; Moneycontrol reported on 23 November at 10:04 IST that the RBI has raised the frequency of portfolio reporting over the last 30–45 days, and that loans against gold stood at **₹3.74 trillion in September 2026, up 66.1% year-on-year**; and Crisil Ratings said on 23 November that it expects gold-loan AUM growth to moderate to about **40% in FY27 from 78% in FY26**.

And the round trip itself: 916 at **₹1,14,450 on 23 November's PM session was +0.85% on 20 November**, and Comex gold rose 3.90% on Monday and fell 2.45% on Tuesday, which puts the dollar price roughly back where it started the week. That is a round trip, not a recovery, and it is stated as one.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: this is the mechanism its book runs on, and it is direction-neutral — the average dampens the damage from a fall and equally dampens the relief from a bounce. No Muthoot Fincorp book is described here, and no lender is named as breaching.

### The week's net move is near zero at the spot level — not at the average level
<!-- tags: topics=gold-loan,regulation; category=analysis -->
Built only on the week's own prints: Comex gold fell **9.47% in the week to 20 November**, settling at the 52-week low of $3,512.30; it then rose **3.90% on 23 November** and fell **2.45% on 24 November**. Two sessions later, the price is roughly a percent and a half above the 20 November low and still 33% below January's record. **The week's net move is close to zero at the spot level — but not at the average level.**

For a gold lender, the relevant number is not either print but the **30-day average**, still carrying October's and early-November's higher prices out of the window while spot sits far below them. That is why the permitted loan value falls even on days when spot rises, and why two sessions of round-tripping change nothing about the LTV arithmetic: the average has barely moved, because neither session was a sustained move.

Silver deserves its own line: a new 52-week low, down **41.88% year-to-date and 16.98% month-to-date**. Silver is not the collateral in a gold loan, but it is the same liquidation dynamic, and it says the selling was not gold-specific.
