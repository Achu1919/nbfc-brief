### Gold-loan NBFCs ask RBI to review capital and risk-weight rules at Kochi summit
<!-- story: id=fgloc-gold-summit-kochi-2026 -->
<!-- tags: companies=iifl-finance,muthoot-finance; regulators=rbi; topics=gold-loan,policy; category=gold -->
The Federation of Gold Loan Companies (FGLOC) held its first Gold Summit in Kochi on Tuesday 17 November and said it will approach RBI for recognition as a Self-Regulatory Organisation (SRO). The industry has asked RBI to review three things. These are representations, not rule changes: the 12% Tier-I capital requirement for gold-loan NBFCs, against 10% for other lending businesses; the 100% risk weight assigned to gold-loan exposures; and the LTV rules.

George Alexander Muthoot, executive vice-chairman of Muthoot Finance (a listed peer, and a separate company from Muthoot Fincorp), said of the capital ask: "It is not a big ask. The same customer, the same gold and the same currency are involved, whether the loan is given by a bank or an NBFC. Banks have a much larger share of the gold-loan market. There should be a level playing field." He also said more than 75% of the gold-loan sector is represented by FGLOC, and urged smaller lenders to join.

On the SRO plan, Pinaki Nath Banerjee, CEO-designate of FGLOC, said the immediate priority is to expand membership before approaching RBI for SRO status. FGLOC has 23 members, with five more still deciding, and is considering changing the shareholding requirement in its articles so that members can join without taking equity. Banerjee acknowledged an existing SRO for NBFCs: "There is an existing SRO for NBFCs. However, the NBFC sector is extremely large, with approximately 9,075 registered companies."

Banerjee put the organised gold-loan market at ₹19–20 lakh crore: ₹5–6 lakh crore with NBFCs and ₹14–15 lakh crore with banks. RBI data cited by Mint show NBFC loans against jewellery up 69% year on year to ₹3.64 trillion at end-August, and the banks' figure up 83% to ₹5.59 trillion.

The summit also brought warnings on growth and auctions. Mayank Sharma, president-lending at IIFL Finance, said: "At the current gold price, there will be a greater focus on customer collections and interest collections." He expects a significant rise in gold auctions this year once prices stabilise over a longer period, and said: "Consequently, we expect growth to remain muted in Q3," as festive and wedding spending and lower prices pressure borrowers' repayment capacity. Banerjee said repayment challenges have emerged after the correction, though fresh loan demand is healthy, and that growth will continue but not at the same magnitude.

Lenders are moving away from short-term bullet repayments towards monthly or quarterly interest and longer tenures, after RBI's revised gold-loan directions, Economic Times reports.

Possible impact on Muthoot Fincorp: Muthoot Fincorp is a gold-loan NBFC, so the asks bear on its sector. They are requests to RBI, not rules, so nothing changes for Muthoot Fincorp from this summit. The sources do not say whether Muthoot Fincorp is an FGLOC member.
